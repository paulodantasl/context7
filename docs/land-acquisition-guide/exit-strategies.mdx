# Attachment 5 — Exit Strategies
## Sell vs. Refinance & Rent | Decision Framework for Brazilian Investors

> Your exit strategy should be decided **before you buy the land** — not after construction ends. The exit determines your financing, your build spec level, and your cash flow projections.

---

## The Two Exits at a Glance

| | **SELL (Flip)** | **REFINANCE & RENT (BRRRR)** |
|--|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Goal** | Maximize profit at sale | Build long-term wealth + recurring income |
| **Timeline** | 12–20 months total | 14–22 months to stabilize |
| **Cash received** | Large lump sum | Partial equity returned + monthly income |
| **Ongoing work** | None after closing | Property management |
| **Tax event** | Capital gains tax at sale | Deferred (depreciation + no sale) |
| **FIRPTA impact** | 15% gross withholding (foreign seller) | None until you eventually sell |
| **Best for** | Investors needing capital back | Investors building a rental portfolio |
| **Risk level** | Market timing risk | Interest rate + tenant risk |

## Exit A: Sell the Property

### When This Makes Sense
- You need to recycle capital quickly into the next deal
- The ARV fully justifies your build costs with 20%+ margin
- Market has strong buyer demand for new construction
- You prefer a clean, defined transaction
- You want to avoid landlord responsibilities
- DSCR math doesn't cash flow adequately to refinance

### The Sell Process

```
CO Issued → List Property → Accept Offer → 30-45 Day Closing → Payoff Construction Loan → Collect Profit
```

**Step 1: Certificate of Occupancy (CO)**
The CO is required before you can sell. Your GC applies for it after final inspection. Timing varies 1–4 weeks after construction completion.

**Step 2: Pre-Listing Prep**
Even for new construction, presentation matters:
- [ ] Professional photography (interior + drone exterior)
- [ ] Staging (can increase sale price 2–5%)
- [ ] Final cleaning
- [ ] Landscaping (sod, mulch, basic curb appeal)
- [ ] Punch list items fully complete

**Step 3: Pricing Strategy**

| Strategy | Description | When to Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Price at ARV | List at or near your appraised value | Strong seller's market |
| Price slightly below | Generate multiple offers, bid up | Competitive market |
| Price with room to negotiate | List 3–5% above target | Slower market |

**Step 4: Contract & Closing**

- Offer acceptance → inspection period (7–14 days)
- Buyer's appraisal (lender requirement)
- Title search & clear to close
- Closing: wire payoff of construction loan, receive net proceeds

---

### Sell Scenario: FIRPTA Explained

**FIRPTA (Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act)** requires the buyer's title company to withhold **15% of the gross sale price** from a foreign seller at closing.

**Example:**
```
Sale Price:           $400,000
FIRPTA Withholding:   × 15% = $60,000
Net to Seller:        $340,000 (before agent fees and other costs)
```

**Important:** FIRPTA withholding is NOT the final tax — it's a prepayment toward your U.S. tax liability. You file a U.S. tax return and may get some or all of it back.

**Ways to Reduce FIRPTA Withholding:**
1. **Withholding Certificate (IRS Form 8288-B)** — Apply to IRS before closing to reduce withholding to your actual estimated tax liability. Takes 3–6 months to process. Plan ahead.
2. **Sell to an owner-occupant at ≤ $300K** — If buyer intends to live in the property and pays ≤ $300K, FIRPTA withholding drops to 0%. ($300K–$1M = 10% withholding)
3. **Use a tax-advantaged entity structure** — consult CPA for details

## Exit B: Refinance & Rent (DSCR)

### When This Makes Sense
- Rental income covers the DSCR loan payments (DSCR ≥ 1.25)
- You want to build a portfolio without selling each deal
- Market rental rates are strong relative to property values
- You have enough cash to absorb what's NOT returned at refi
- Long-term wealth building is the goal over short-term profit
- You want to defer capital gains tax

### The DSCR Refi Process

```
CO Issued → Place Tenants (or list) → Apply for DSCR Loan → Appraisal → Close Refi → Pay Off Construction Loan → Monthly Cash Flow
```

**Step 1: Get Certificate of Occupancy**
Required before any permanent financing can close.

**Step 2: Stabilize the Property**

Most DSCR lenders want:
- Property leased (or leases executed, not yet started)
- OR: Vacant "as-stabilized" appraisal is acceptable with many lenders

**Tip:** For multifamily (2–4 units), having signed leases before applying for the DSCR loan demonstrates rental income and improves your DSCR qualification.

**Step 3: DSCR Loan Application**

Documentation needed:
- Executed lease agreement(s)
- Certificate of Occupancy
- Property appraisal (lender orders)
- LLC documents + ITIN
- 3–6 months U.S. bank statements
- Evidence of construction loan payoff plan

**Step 4: Appraisal**

The lender orders an "as-is" appraisal of the completed property. This becomes your LTV basis for the DSCR loan. If the appraisal comes in low:
- You may receive less than expected from the refi
- You'll need more cash to pay off the construction loan

**Build to a conservative ARV** — don't count on top-of-market appraisals.

**Step 5: Close the DSCR Refi**

At closing:
- DSCR loan funds
- Construction loan is paid off in full
- Any excess funds go to you (equity return)
- Monthly payments on DSCR loan begin

## Comparing Both Exits Side-by-Side

Use this framework for your specific deal numbers.

| Metric | Sell | Refi & Rent |
|--------|------|-------------|
| Immediate cash received | High | Medium (partial) |
| Ongoing monthly income | $0 | $200–$600/month |
| Capital gains tax | Due now | Deferred |
| FIRPTA withholding | Yes (15%) | No |
| Equity retained | 0 | Yes (~30%) |
| Management required | No | Yes (or PM fee) |
| Next deal capital | Fast recycle | Partial recycle |

## Decision Framework: Which Exit to Choose?

### Choose SELL if any of these apply:
- [ ] DSCR < 1.0 — rental income doesn't cover the loan
- [ ] You need capital back for the next deal immediately
- [ ] Local rental market is weak (high vacancy, falling rents)
- [ ] Interest rates make the DSCR payment too high to cash flow
- [ ] You can achieve 25%+ gross margin at sale
- [ ] You want to avoid ongoing property management

### Choose REFINANCE & RENT if all of these apply:
- [ ] DSCR ≥ 1.25 — property cash flows after refi
- [ ] Monthly cash flow positive after all expenses
- [ ] You have reserves to handle the cash not returned at refi
- [ ] Local rental demand is strong (low vacancy, stable rents)
- [ ] You want to build long-term equity and rental income
- [ ] You plan to hold the property 5+ years

### Hybrid Strategy: Build to Sell, Pivot to Refi
Some investors build with the intent to sell, but if the market softens during construction, they refinance and hold. This requires:
- Adequate construction loan extension options
- Ability to qualify for DSCR refi
- Financial reserves to absorb cash flow shortfalls

## Long-Term Portfolio Building (BRRRR at Scale)

If you successfully complete the BRRRR cycle (Build, Rent, Refinance, Repeat), here's how a portfolio grows:

### Year 1
- Build SFR #1 → Refi → Hold
- Cash returned: $40,000
- Remaining equity: $80,000

### Year 2
- Use returned cash + reserves for Deal #2
- Build Duplex → Refi → Hold
- Growing rental income from Property #1

### Year 3–5
- Each successful BRRRR returns partial capital for reinvestment
- Monthly cash flow compounds with each property added
- Depreciation offsets rental income for U.S. tax purposes

**10-Unit Target (5 Duplexes over 5 years):**
```
Properties:        5 duplexes
Total Value:       ~$2,500,000
Total Debt:        ~$1,750,000 (70% LTV)
Total Equity:      ~$750,000
Monthly Rent:      ~$15,000
Monthly Expenses:  ~$13,500
Monthly Cash Flow: ~$1,500–$3,000
Annual Cash Flow:  ~$18,000–$36,000
```

## Selling a Portfolio (Long-Term Exit)

When you eventually sell a rental portfolio:
- **1031 Exchange**: Defer capital gains by reinvesting proceeds in another investment property (not available to foreign nationals without careful structuring — consult CPA)
- **Installment Sale**: Spread gains over time to reduce annual tax liability
- **Sell individual properties**: Stagger sales across tax years
- **FIRPTA planning**: File Form 8288-B early for each sale
