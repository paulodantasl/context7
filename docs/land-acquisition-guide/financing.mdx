# Attachment 2 — Financing Guide for Brazilian Investors
## Ground-Up Development | Foreign National Loan Programs

> Brazilian investors do not need a U.S. Social Security Number (SSN) or U.S. credit history to obtain financing. This guide covers the loan programs available to foreign nationals building ground-up in the U.S.

---

## The Two Loan Phases

Ground-up development requires **two separate financing events**:

```
PHASE 1: CONSTRUCTION LOAN              PHASE 2: EXIT FINANCING
─────────────────────────────           ─────────────────────────────
Short-term (12–18 months)               Triggered after Certificate
Interest-only draws                     of Occupancy (CO) is issued
Covers: Land + Construction
                                        Option A: SELL
                                        → Pay off construction loan
                                        → Collect profit

                                        Option B: REFI (DSCR Loan)
                                        → Replace construction loan
                                        → Long-term rental mortgage
                                        → Pull equity out (BRRRR)
```

## Part 1: Construction Loans for Foreign Nationals

### What Is a Construction Loan?

A construction loan is a **short-term, interest-only loan** that funds the building process. Unlike a traditional mortgage, funds are disbursed in **draws** as construction milestones are completed.

- Typical term: **12–18 months**
- Interest rate: **9%–13%** (as of 2024–2025)
- Loan-to-Cost (LTC): **70%–80%** of total project cost
- Loan-to-ARV: typically **65%–70%** of appraised value after completion
- Interest accrues only on drawn funds (not the full loan amount)

### Who Lends to Foreign Nationals?

Most conventional banks **will not** lend to foreign nationals without a U.S. SSN and established U.S. credit. The following lender types **do** work with foreign nationals:

#### Hard Money Lenders (Most Accessible)
- Asset-based lending — your deal's numbers matter more than your credit score
- Faster closing (2–4 weeks)
- Higher rates (10%–14%)
- Points: 2–4 origination points
- Typically require 20%–30% down
- Best for: first-time builders, smaller deals

**Example lenders:** Kiavi, Lima One Capital, Visio Lending, RCN Capital, many local/regional hard money lenders

#### Foreign National Construction Loan Programs
- Specialized programs designed for non-U.S. residents
- Rates: 9%–12%
- LTC: 65%–75%
- Require: valid passport, ITIN, U.S. LLC, U.S. bank account
- Some require in-person closing or notarized documents from Brazil

**Example lenders:** A&D Mortgage, Angel Oak, Quontic Bank, some credit unions

#### Portfolio Lenders / Community Banks
- Small to mid-size banks that hold loans on their balance sheet
- Sometimes more flexible on foreign national guidelines
- Relationship-based — worth building a relationship with a local banker
- Rates typically between hard money and conventional

### Construction Loan Requirements for Foreign Nationals

#### Standard Documentation
| Document | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Valid passport | Must be current |
| ITIN (W-7) | Apply via IRS — allow 7–11 weeks |
| U.S. LLC documents | Articles of Organization, Operating Agreement |
| U.S. business bank account statements | 3–6 months, show funds for down payment |
| Source of funds | May need to show foreign bank statements proving BRL → USD transfer |
| Executed purchase contract (land) | Or already closed land deed if you own it |
| Construction contract | From licensed GC — fixed-price preferred |
| Architectural plans | Permitted plans preferred |
| Construction budget | Detailed line-item budget from GC |
| Appraisal | Lender will order — "as-completed" appraisal |
| 2 years of tax returns | Brazilian returns accepted by some lenders; others require U.S. returns |
| CPA letter | Some lenders accept letter verifying income/assets |

#### Financial Requirements
- **Down payment / equity:** 20%–35% of total project cost
- **Reserves:** 6–12 months of interest payments in U.S. bank account
- **No U.S. credit score required** (asset-based underwriting)
- **Minimum deal size:** Most lenders start at $75K–$150K loan amount
- **Property must be non-owner-occupied** (investment property)

---

### Construction Loan Draw Process

Construction loans are NOT disbursed all at once. Funds are released in **draws** tied to construction milestones:

| Draw | Milestone | Typical % of Loan |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Draw 1 | Foundation complete | 10%–15% |
| Draw 2 | Framing complete | 15%–20% |
| Draw 3 | Rough-in MEP (plumbing, electric, HVAC) | 15%–20% |
| Draw 4 | Insulation, drywall, exterior | 15%–20% |
| Draw 5 | Interior finishes, flooring, fixtures | 15%–20% |
| Draw 6 | Final completion, CO issued | 10%–15% |

**How draws work:**
1. GC completes a milestone
2. You submit a draw request with documentation / photos
3. Lender sends inspector to verify work
4. Lender releases funds to your account (usually 3–7 business days)
5. You pay the GC from released funds

**Important:** You typically need cash to fund work **before** the draw is released. Coordinate with your GC on payment timing.

## Part 2: Exit Financing Options

### Option A: Sell the Property

When construction is complete and you have a Certificate of Occupancy:
1. List property with real estate agent
2. Accept offer, go under contract
3. Buyer obtains their own financing (or pays cash)
4. Close, pay off construction loan, collect profit

**For FIRPTA purposes:** As a foreign national selling U.S. real property, the buyer's title company will withhold **15% of the gross sale price** for FIRPTA. You can apply for a withholding certificate to reduce this if your actual tax liability is lower. Consult your CPA.

---

### Option B: DSCR Refinance (Debt Service Coverage Ratio Loan)

A DSCR loan is a **long-term rental mortgage** that qualifies based on the property's rental income — not your personal income. This is ideal for Brazilian investors without U.S. W-2 income.

#### How DSCR Works
```
DSCR = Gross Monthly Rent ÷ Monthly PITIA (Principal + Interest + Tax + Insurance + HOA)

DSCR ≥ 1.0 = rent covers debt service (breakeven)
DSCR ≥ 1.25 = most lenders' minimum requirement
DSCR ≥ 1.35 = stronger approval, better rates
```

#### DSCR Loan Parameters (Foreign National Programs)
| Parameter | Typical Range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Minimum DSCR | 1.0–1.25 |
| Loan-to-Value (LTV) | 65%–75% |
| Interest rate | 7%–10% (30-year fixed) |
| Loan term | 30 years |
| Minimum loan | $75,000–$150,000 |
| ITIN accepted | Yes (with most foreign national programs) |
| U.S. SSN required | No |
| U.S. credit score required | Sometimes (or alternative credit) |

#### DSCR Refinance Example

| Item | SFR | Duplex |
|------|-----|--------|
| Completed property value (ARV) | $350,000 | $520,000 |
| DSCR loan at 70% LTV | $245,000 | $364,000 |
| Monthly rent (market) | $2,200 | $3,600 ($1,800/unit) |
| Monthly P&I (30yr @ 8%) | $1,798 | $2,670 |
| Monthly taxes | $350 | $500 |
| Monthly insurance | $150 | $220 |
| Total PITIA | $2,298 | $3,390 |
| **DSCR** | **0.96** ⚠️ | **1.06** ✓ |
| Monthly cash flow | ($98) | $210 |

*Note: SFR at these numbers barely qualifies. 2-4 unit typically has stronger DSCR. Always run numbers before building.*

## Part 3: The BRRRR Method (Build, Rent, Refinance, Repeat)

Ground-up construction is essentially a **"Build" version of the BRRRR strategy**:

```
BUILD         RENT         REFINANCE       REPEAT
──────        ──────       ──────────      ──────
Buy land   →  Tenant     → DSCR refi    → Take equity
+ build        occupied     pays off        out and
               (stabilize)  construction    fund next
                            loan            project
```

### BRRRR Math Example

| Step | Numbers |
|------|---------|
| Land cost | $80,000 |
| Construction cost | $210,000 |
| Closing + financing costs | $30,000 |
| **Total cash invested** | **$320,000** |
| Completed value (ARV) | $420,000 |
| DSCR refi at 70% LTV | $294,000 |
| Construction loan payoff | $225,000 |
| **Cash returned after refi** | **$69,000** |
| **Remaining equity in deal** | **$126,000** |
| Monthly rent | $2,500 |
| Monthly PITIA | $2,100 |
| **Monthly cash flow** | **$400** |
| **Cash left in deal (net)** | **$251,000** (original $320K − $69K returned) |

## Financing Timeline

| Week | Activity |
|------|---------|
| Week 1–2 | Contact 3–5 lenders, submit pre-qualification |
| Week 3–4 | Get pre-approval letter (before going under contract on land) |
| Week 5–8 | Under contract on land — submit full loan application |
| Week 8–12 | Appraisal ordered, plans reviewed, loan approved |
| Week 12–14 | Close on land and construction loan simultaneously |
| Month 2–14 | Construction draws as milestones are hit |
| Month 14–16 | CO received — begin exit (sell or DSCR refi process) |

## Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| LTC | Loan-to-Cost — loan amount ÷ total project cost |
| LTV | Loan-to-Value — loan amount ÷ appraised property value |
| ARV | After Repair/Build Value — expected value when complete |
| DSCR | Debt Service Coverage Ratio — rent ÷ total monthly debt |
| Draw | Disbursement of construction loan funds at a milestone |
| Points | Origination fee = % of loan amount (1 point = 1%) |
| Hard Money | Short-term asset-based loan, higher rate, faster closing |
| Non-QM | Non-Qualified Mortgage — alternative income documentation |
| ITIN | Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (for non-SSN filers) |
| FIRPTA | Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act — withholding on sale |
| CO | Certificate of Occupancy — permits occupancy, triggers exit financing |
