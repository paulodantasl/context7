# Attachment 6 — Legal & Tax Guide for Foreign Investors
## Brazilian Nationals Investing in U.S. Ground-Up Development

> **Disclaimer:** This guide provides general educational information only. It is not legal or tax advice. Always consult a licensed U.S. attorney and CPA with experience in foreign national real estate investment before making any decisions.

---

## Part 1: Getting Your Legal Foundation in Order

### Step 1: Obtain an ITIN

An **ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number)** is your U.S. tax ID as a foreign national without a Social Security Number. You need it to:
- Open a U.S. bank account (some banks)
- File U.S. tax returns
- Apply for certain loans
- Be listed on an LLC

**How to Apply:**
1. Complete **IRS Form W-7**
2. Attach a certified copy of your passport (or other accepted ID)
3. Submit with a U.S. tax return (or qualifying exception) to:
   - IRS Austin Service Center
   - OR through an **IRS Acceptance Agent** (easier — they handle certification)
   - OR through a **Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA)** who can certify documents in Brazil

**Timeline:** 7–11 weeks from submission

**Validity:** ITINs expire if not used on a tax return for 3 consecutive years, or every 3 years for certain assigned ranges. Renew as needed.

**IRS Resource:** irs.gov/itin

---

### Step 2: Form a U.S. LLC

A **Limited Liability Company (LLC)** is the standard structure for U.S. real estate investment. For foreign nationals, it provides:
- **Liability protection** — your personal assets are separate from the property
- **Pass-through taxation** — profits taxed at your individual level, not double-taxed
- **Professional credibility** — lenders and title companies prefer LLCs for investment property

#### Which State to Form the LLC In?

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **State where you're investing** (most common) | Simple — one state, no foreign registration needed | Varies by state |
| **Wyoming** | Low cost, strong privacy laws, no state income tax | Must register as foreign LLC in investing state |
| **Delaware** | Preferred for business sophistication, flexible law | Must register in investing state, adds cost |
| **Florida / Texas** | Good if investing there, no state income tax | Less privacy protection than Wyoming |

**Recommendation for most Brazilian investors:** Form the LLC in the state where you're investing. It's simpler and avoids extra foreign registration filings.

#### LLC Formation Checklist

- [ ] Choose LLC name (check availability with state Secretary of State)
- [ ] File **Articles of Organization** with Secretary of State
- [ ] Draft **Operating Agreement** (critical — establishes ownership + rules)
- [ ] Obtain **EIN (Employer Identification Number)** from IRS (Form SS-4, can apply online)
- [ ] Open U.S. **business bank account** in LLC name
- [ ] Register for state/local business licenses if required

**Cost:** $50–$500 filing fee depending on state + attorney fees if using one

#### Single-Member vs. Multi-Member LLC

| Structure | U.S. Tax Treatment | Foreign Owner Implication |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Single-member LLC** (SMLLC) | Disregarded entity — taxed on your personal return | Simple; files Schedule E |
| **Multi-member LLC** | Partnership — files Form 1065 | More complex; each member files K-1 |
| **LLC taxed as S-Corp** | Not available to non-resident aliens | ❌ Cannot use |
| **LLC taxed as C-Corp** | Double taxation possible | Sometimes used for complex structures |

**Most common for Brazilian investors:** Single-member LLC owned by the individual (or by a Brazilian holding company for additional planning).

### Step 3: Open a U.S. Bank Account

You need a U.S. business bank account to:
- Receive and make construction loan draws
- Collect rental income
- Pay contractors, taxes, insurance
- Demonstrate funds to lenders

#### Banks That Work with Foreign Nationals / LLCs

| Bank | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **Mercury** | Online bank, great for LLCs, ITIN accepted in some cases |
| **Relay Financial** | Business banking, ITIN-friendly |
| **Chase** | Requires in-person branch visit, may require SSN or additional docs |
| **Bank of America** | May work with passport + ITIN + LLC docs |
| **Community banks / credit unions** | Often more flexible; good for relationship lending |
| **Silicon Valley Bank / East West Bank** | Good for internationally-owned LLCs |

**Required documents (typical):**

- ITIN (some banks)

## Part 2: U.S. Tax Obligations for Brazilian Real Estate Investors

### Overview of Applicable Taxes

| Tax | When It Applies | Rate |
|-----|----------------|------|
| Federal income tax (rental income) | Annually on net rental income | 10%–37% (ordinary rates) |
| Capital gains tax (property sale) | When you sell | 15%–20% (long-term) |
| FIRPTA withholding | At closing when foreign person sells | 15% of gross sale price |
| State income tax | Depends on state | 0%–13% |
| Property tax | Annually (local) | 0.5%–2.5% of assessed value |
| Self-employment tax | Only if active trade/business | 15.3% on net income |

---

### FIRPTA — The Most Important Tax for Foreign Sellers

**FIRPTA (Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act)** was designed to ensure the U.S. collects taxes when foreign nationals sell U.S. real estate.

**How it works:**
1. You (foreign seller) list and sell property
2. Buyer's title company withholds **15% of gross sale price** at closing
3. Title company remits to IRS within 20 days
4. You file U.S. tax return and reconcile actual tax vs. withholding
5. If withholding > actual tax: **you receive a refund**
6. If withholding < actual tax: you pay the difference

**FIRPTA Withholding Table:**

| Sale Price | Buyer Use | Withholding Rate |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| ≤ $300,000 | Buyer is owner-occupant | 0% |
| $300,001–$1,000,000 | Buyer is owner-occupant | 10% |
| Any price | Investment / rental purchase | 15% |
| Any price | Foreign seller using Withholding Certificate | Reduced rate |

**Withholding Certificate (Form 8288-B):**
- Apply to IRS before closing to reduce withholding
- IRS must approve; takes 3–6 months
- Reduces withholding to estimated actual tax liability
- File this early — plan ahead

**Where to Get Help:** Your CPA will handle FIRPTA filings. This is a standard service for foreign national real estate CPAs.

---

### Annual U.S. Tax Returns

As a foreign national owning U.S. investment real estate, you must file:

| Form | Purpose | Due Date |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Form 1040-NR** | Non-resident alien income tax return | April 15 (or Oct 15 with extension) |
| **Schedule E** | Rental income and expenses | Attached to 1040-NR |
| **Form 8825** (if LLC files as partnership) | Rental income for partnerships | March 15 |
| **Form 1065** (if multi-member LLC) | Partnership information return | March 15 |
| **FinCEN 114 (FBAR)** | If U.S. bank account balance > $10,000 | April 15 |

**Important:** Even if you have no income in a given year, you may still need to file. Work with a CPA annually.

### Depreciation — Your Tax Advantage for Rentals

When you hold a rental property, the IRS allows you to **deduct the cost of the building** over time through **depreciation**. This reduces your taxable rental income, often to zero or below.

**Residential Real Estate Depreciation:**
- **Building value** (not land) is depreciated over **27.5 years**
- Deduction = Building value ÷ 27.5 per year
- Land is NOT depreciable

**Example:**
```
Property value:         $400,000
Land value (est. 20%):  $80,000
Building value:         $320,000
Annual depreciation:    $320,000 ÷ 27.5 = $11,636/year
```

If your rental income is $30,000/year and expenses (mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, PM) are $20,000:
```
Net rental income:      $10,000
Depreciation deduction: ($11,636)
Taxable income:         $0 (or small loss)
```

**Bonus Depreciation & Cost Segregation:**
- A **cost segregation study** ($3,000–$10,000) accelerates depreciation on personal property components (appliances, fixtures, improvements)
- This can front-load large deductions in year 1 of ownership
- Ask your CPA about current bonus depreciation rules

## Part 3: Entity Structure Options

### Structure 1: Individual ITIN (Simplest, Not Recommended)

```
Paulo (Brazilian national, ITIN)
        │
        └──→ Owns property directly
```

- **Pros:** Simple, no entity cost
- **Cons:** Personal liability, no asset protection, harder to get loans, tax complications

---

### Structure 2: Single U.S. LLC (Recommended Starting Point)

```
Paulo (Brazilian national, ITIN)
        │
        └──→ Owns 100% of U.S. LLC (Florida/Texas/etc.)
                        │
                        └──→ Owns property
```

- **Pros:** Liability protection, pass-through taxation, lender-friendly
- **Cons:** Minimal additional privacy, limited planning for multiple properties

---

### Structure 3: One LLC Per Property (Asset Protection)

```
Paulo (Brazilian national)
        │
        └──→ Holding LLC (Wyoming)
                    │
                    ├──→ Property LLC #1 (Florida) — owns Property 1
                    ├──→ Property LLC #2 (Florida) — owns Property 2
                    └──→ Property LLC #3 (Florida) — owns Property 3
```

- **Pros:** Each property isolated — a lawsuit on Property 1 can't reach Property 2
- **Cons:** Higher cost (multiple LLCs + registered agents + annual fees)
- **Best for:** Portfolio of 3+ properties

---

### Structure 4: Brazilian Holding Company → U.S. LLC (Advanced)

```
Brazilian Holding Company (Ltda. or S.A.)
        │
        └──→ U.S. LLC
                │
                └──→ U.S. Investment Properties
```

- **Pros:** Can simplify Brazilian inheritance law, potential treaty benefits
- **Cons:** Complex, requires coordination between Brazilian and U.S. attorneys and CPAs
- **Best for:** Investors with significant capital, family planning considerations

**Caution:** This structure requires expert guidance. Brazil and the U.S. do not have a comprehensive tax treaty — careful structuring is required to avoid double taxation.

## Part 4: Brazil–U.S. Tax Considerations

### No Comprehensive Tax Treaty

Brazil and the United States **do not have a comprehensive income tax treaty**. This means:
- Income taxed in the U.S. may also be taxable in Brazil
- Foreign tax credits may apply to reduce double taxation
- Consult BOTH a U.S. CPA and a Brazilian tax advisor (contador)

### Declaring U.S. Assets in Brazil

Brazilian residents must declare **foreign assets** with **Receita Federal (Brazilian IRS)**:
- **DIRPF** — Annual individual tax return includes foreign assets section
- **CBE (Capital Brasileiro no Exterior)** — Banco Central requires declaration if foreign assets > $1,000,000 USD
- Failure to declare can result in significant penalties

### Currency Exchange & Remittance

When transferring funds from Brazil to the U.S. for investment:
- Use a licensed **câmbio (exchange)** — don't use informal channels
- Major banks: Itaú, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil offer international wire services
- FX brokers (OFX, Wise Business) offer competitive rates for larger transfers
- Document all transfers — you'll need to show source of funds to U.S. lenders and for Brazilian Receita Federal

## Part 5: Insurance Requirements

| Insurance Type | When Required | Typical Cost |
|----------------|--------------|-------------|
| **Builder's Risk** | During construction (required by lender) | $1,500–$5,000/year |
| **General Liability (via GC)** | During construction (your GC carries this) | GC's cost |
| **Homeowners Insurance** | At DSCR refi closing (required) | $1,200–$3,000/year |
| **Landlord / Rental Property Insurance** | If renting (required by DSCR lender) | $1,200–$3,000/year |
| **Flood Insurance** | If in flood zone (required by lender) | $500–$3,000+/year |
| **Umbrella Policy** | Recommended for portfolio owners | $300–$1,000/year |
| **Title Insurance** | At closing (owner's + lender's) | 0.5%–1% of property value (one-time) |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem |
|---------|-------------------|
| Buying property in your personal name (no LLC) | Personal liability, harder to finance, messy tax situation |
| Not having ITIN before starting | Delays financing, closing, and tax filings |
| Ignoring FIRPTA planning until day of closing | 15% withholding surprises; no time to file 8288-B |
| Not declaring U.S. assets in Brazil | Brazilian tax penalties and legal risk |
| Using informal currency transfers | Cannot document source of funds for lenders or tax purposes |
| One LLC for multiple properties | A lawsuit against one property can reach all others |
| No Operating Agreement | Disputes, lender complications, legal vulnerability |
| No U.S. tax return because "I don't live there" | You have U.S.-source income — you must file |
