## Who This Is For

- Brazilian nationals investing in U.S. real estate
- Investors without a U.S. Social Security Number (SSN) — using ITIN
- Those targeting **ground-up construction** rather than buying existing homes
- Investors seeking one of two exits: **sell for profit** or **refinance and hold for rental income**

---

## Property Types Covered

### Single Family Residence (SFR)
- 1 unit, typically 3/2 or 4/2
- Easiest to permit, finance, and sell
- Lower total investment
- Larger buyer pool at exit
- Best for: first-time builders, smaller markets

### 2–4 Unit Multi-Family
- Duplex (2), Triplex (3), or Quadplex (4)
- Financed as **residential** (conventional/DSCR loans available)
- Higher construction cost, higher rental income
- More attractive for refinance & hold strategy
- Best for: cash flow focus, experienced investors

---

## Key Differences: SFR vs. 2–4 Unit

| Factor | SFR | 2–4 Unit |
|--------|-----|----------|
| Avg. build cost | $150K–$350K | $250K–$600K |
| Permitting complexity | Low | Medium |
| Financing options | Broad | Broad (still residential) |
| Buyer pool at sale | Large | Smaller (investor-focused) |
| Monthly rental income | $1,200–$2,500 | $2,400–$6,000+ |
| DSCR refinance viability | Medium | High |
| Best exit | Sell | Refi & Rent |

## Why Ground-Up Development?

### Advantages Over Buying Existing Properties
- **No overpaying** for renovated homes in a competitive market
- **Full control** over layout, specs, and quality
- **Higher margins** in markets where new construction commands premium pricing
- **Modern construction** = lower maintenance, higher appraisal values
- **Depreciation benefits** for long-term holds (tax advantage)

### Challenges to Understand
- Longer timeline (12–24 months vs. 30–60 days for a buy/sell)
- Construction loan requirements are stricter for foreign nationals
- Permitting delays are common — budget for them
- You are managing a project, not just buying an asset
- Currency exchange risk (BRL/USD) over a multi-month build period

## U.S. Market Selection for Ground-Up Development

### Top Criteria
1. **Lot availability and affordability** — can you buy land at a viable price?
2. **Cost of construction** — varies significantly by state and city
3. **New construction demand** — is there a buyer/renter market?
4. **Permitting speed** — some cities take 3 months, others 12+
5. **Investor-friendly environment** — landlord laws, property taxes, HOA rules

### Recommended Market Categories for Brazilian Investors

#### Tier 1 — Most Accessible Entry Points
| Market | State | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Jacksonville | FL | No state income tax, affordable land, growing population |
| San Antonio | TX | Fast permitting, affordable lots, strong job growth |
| Memphis | TN | Low land cost, high rental demand, no state income tax |
| Indianapolis | IN | Affordable construction, stable rental market |
| Columbus | OH | Growing city, lower competition, reasonable land prices |

#### Tier 2 — Higher Potential, Higher Cost
| Market | State | Why |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Tampa / St. Pete | FL | Strong appreciation, high demand for new builds |
| Austin suburbs | TX | Premium pricing for new construction |
| Charlotte | NC | Fast growth, infrastructure investment |
| Nashville suburbs | TN | High income renters, strong appreciation |

### What to Avoid (for Beginners)
- **High-regulation states**: California, New York, Massachusetts (slow permits, high costs)
- **Markets with falling population**: Detroit, Chicago outer areas
- **Flood zones without flood insurance analysis**
- **Areas with HOA restrictions on construction or rentals**

## Brazilian Investor Essentials

### Before You Start
- [ ] Obtain a U.S. **ITIN** (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number)
- [ ] Open a **U.S. business bank account** (usually requires LLC first)
- [ ] Form a **U.S. LLC** (ideally in the state where you're investing)
- [ ] Consult a **CPA experienced with foreign investors** (FIRPTA, depreciation, etc.)
- [ ] Understand current **BRL/USD exchange rate** and hedging options
- [ ] Engage a **Real Estate Attorney** for entity structure and closings

### ITIN vs. SSN
Brazilian investors typically use an **ITIN**, not an SSN. This affects:
- Which lenders will work with you (foreign national loan programs)
- How you file U.S. taxes
- Credit underwriting (lenders use asset-based / DSCR underwriting instead)

## Quick-Start Checklist

### Month 1–2: Setup
- [ ] Form LLC in target state
- [ ] Apply for ITIN (allow 7–11 weeks)
- [ ] Open U.S. bank account
- [ ] Identify 2–3 target markets
- [ ] Connect with local real estate agent (land specialist)
- [ ] Connect with construction lender (foreign national programs)
- [ ] Interview 2–3 general contractors in target market

### Month 2–4: Land Acquisition
- [ ] Run land comps and feasibility analysis
- [ ] Submit offer on land
- [ ] Complete due diligence (see [Due Diligence Checklist](/land-acquisition-guide/land-due-diligence))
- [ ] Close on land

### Month 3–5: Pre-Construction
- [ ] Hire architect / draftsman
- [ ] Submit plans for permit
- [ ] Get construction loan approved
- [ ] Finalize contractor contract

### Month 5–17: Construction
- [ ] Break ground
- [ ] Weekly site visits or inspection reports
- [ ] Draw schedule management
- [ ] Certificate of Occupancy (CO)

### Month 17–20: Exit
- [ ] List for sale OR apply for DSCR refinance
- [ ] Close and distribute proceeds
- [ ] File U.S. taxes (FIRPTA withholding if selling)
